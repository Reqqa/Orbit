## Auto-match rate

Threshold: a top candidate scoring 90 or more out of 100.

| Outcome | Count | Share |
|---|---:|---:|
| Top candidate >= 90 and it is the labelled invoice | 22 | 44.0% |
| Top candidate >= 90 and it is the WRONG invoice | 0 | 0.0% |
| No candidate reached 90 (routed to a human) | 28 | 56.0% |

Auto-match rate: **22/50 (44.0%)** of all receipts, or **22/45 (48.9%)** of the receipts that have a labelled invoice at all. The second denominator excludes the 5 receipts that are not a payment against a known invoice (the not-a-receipt images and the unknown-payer transfers), which can never auto-match by definition.

The score and the classification are separate: a receipt can score 100 on the right invoice and still be held for a human, because a low per-field confidence sends it to LOW_CONFIDENCE whatever the score. That is the intended direction to fail in.

**No confident wrong answers:** no receipt scored 90 or more on an invoice other than the labelled one.
A confident wrong answer is the expensive failure mode in a payments
product, so it is counted separately whether or not any occur.

No payment is recorded without a human approval, whatever the score
(CLAUDE.md non-negotiable 5).

## Caveats

- With the mock extractor the extraction numbers are 100% BY CONSTRUCTION, because the mock reads the label file. This is not a measurement of the model. Run with --provider claude to measure the model.
- The receipts, customers and invoices are synthetic and generated from a
  deterministic seed. These numbers describe that test set only.
- Each receipt is evaluated on its own against the ledger as it stands.
  Duplicate detection needs the earlier copy of the payment to be recorded
  already, and this evaluation approves nothing, so a receipt labelled
  DUPLICATE is only classified DUPLICATE here when that earlier payment is
  already in the database. The DUPLICATE row of the confusion matrix
  therefore understates what the running system does.
